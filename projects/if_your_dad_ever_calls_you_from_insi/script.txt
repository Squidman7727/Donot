You're sound asleep. The phone rings. It’s your dad. He's calling from inside the house. You need to answer it. Do not let it go to voicemail. Do not try to call him back on his actual number. And for the love of everything, do not go looking for him.

I’m not talking about a prank call. I'm not talking about a drunk dial from the living room. I’m talking about this. You’re in bed. The house is quiet. Your phone vibrates. It’s "Dad." The caller ID clearly states "Inside House." A deep dread settles in your stomach. You can hear his muffled voice through the thin walls, calling your name. Or is it? A whisper. A scrape. You know he's not usually up at this hour.

You pick up. The line is dead silent. Nothing. You hear a soft, rhythmic *thump… thump… thump* from downstairs. You hold your breath. Then a voice, distorted, like it’s underwater, says your name. It’s not quite your dad. You hang up. A mistake. A terrible mistake.

There are seven rules you must follow if this ever happens. Rule seven explains why this is happening. And why you're already in so much danger.

Rule number one. If your dad ever calls you from inside your house at 3 AM… ANSWER IT. You'll want to ignore it. You'll want to silence your phone and pretend you're still asleep. You think if you don't answer, it'll go away. It won't. What's really happening is it’s testing you. It needs to know you're aware. It needs to know you're listening. People who've tried to ignore it say the calls just keep coming. Louder. More insistent. Eventually, they hear a knocking on their bedroom door. A soft, insistent scratching. Answer the phone. Immediately.

Rule number two. DO NOT HANG UP. You'll be tempted. The silence will be unnerving. The distorted voice will make your skin crawl. You'll think it's a glitch, a bad connection. You'll want to end the call and try again. Don't. What's really happening is it's establishing a connection. It's creating a tether between you and it. Breaking that connection is like cutting a life support. People who've tried to hang up say the phone immediately rings again. This time, the caller ID says "DAD – IN YOUR ROOM." A young woman named Sarah tried to hang up. Her phone immediately rang. The voice on the other end said, "You shouldn't have done that, Sarah." She felt a cold breath on her neck. Keep the line open. No matter what.

Rule number three. DO NOT SPEAK FIRST. You'll want to ask, "Dad? What's wrong?" You'll want to break the silence, to understand. You'll want to reassure yourself that it's just a strange dream. Don't. What's really happening is it's listening. It's learning your voice. It's mapping your inflections. People who've tried to speak first say the voice on the other end perfectly mimicked their own. Then it started asking them questions. Personal questions. Too personal. Wait for it to speak.

Rule number four. DO NOT LET IT SPEAK FOR MORE THAN TEN SECONDS. You'll be paralyzed by fear. The garbled sounds, the strange clicks and whispers will hold you captive. You'll want to understand what it's saying. Don't. What's really happening is it’s feeding. It’s gaining strength with every word. Every second it speaks, it becomes more real. People who've tried to listen for too long say they started to feel weak, drained. Their vision blurred. Their thoughts became fuzzy. When it starts speaking, interrupt it before ten seconds are up.

Rule number five. INTERRUPT IT WITH A QUESTION ABOUT YOUR CHILDHOOD. You'll be panicked. Your mind will be racing. You'll struggle to think of anything to say. You'll want to scream. Don't. What's really happening is it's vulnerable to specific memories. Memories that are deeply personal and tied to your real father. People who've tried other questions say it just laughed. A cold, hollow sound. A man named Mark remembered his first baseball game with his dad. He asked, "Remember that time we went to the Yankees game?" The garbled voice on the phone went silent. Then it said, "I remember." But it was a different voice. A human voice. A real voice.

Rule number six. IF IT ANSWERS CORRECTLY, ASK IT TO HUM YOUR LULLABY. You'll be relieved. You'll think you've broken through. You'll think it's your dad. Don't. What's really happening is it’s trying to trick you. It’s mimicking your father's memories, but it can't mimic his soul. People who've tried to believe it say they heard a sickening crunch. A wet, tearing sound. Your dad would know your lullaby. He’d hum it perfectly. It can’t. It won’t. If it tries, it will fail. It will make a sound like static. Or a choked gurgle. This is your chance.

Rule number seven. WHEN IT FAILS TO HUM THE LULLABY, HANG UP AND THROW YOUR PHONE OUT THE WINDOW. You'll think you're safe. You'll think it's over. You'll want to keep your phone. Don't. What's really happening is the phone is merely a conduit. A temporary anchor. It’s inside. It’s always been inside. That quiet creak you hear when you're home alone. That shadow you catch from the corner of your eye. That feeling you get that you're never truly by yourself. It’s all been it. It’s been here all along. Growing. Waiting. And the phone call? It's just its way of letting you know it's finally ready. Ready to move from the shadows into your room. Ready to replace your dad. Ready to replace you.

Every strange phone call you've ever gotten. Every time your dad's phone died at the most inconvenient moment. Every time you thought you heard footsteps when no one was there. It was always there. It was always watching. It was always listening. You just didn't know it had a number.
