If your best friend texts you "I'm outside"... don't look out the window. Not tonight. Not ever.

This isn't a game. This is real. People disappear.

They say there are seven rules. Six will keep you safe until morning. The seventh is the one almost nobody finds in time.

Ten. Nine. Eight. Seven. Six. Five. Four. Three. Two. One.

Don't look out the window. Just don't.

Leo was eleven. It was 9:14 PM on a Tuesday. Pizza night. His mom had called him down twice.

[shout] Leo! Dinner! Come on down!

He was halfway down the stairs when his phone buzzed. A text. From Liam. His best friend.

"I'm outside."

Leo paused. Liam never visited on weeknights. Especially not this late.

Rule one. Don't answer the voice.

The voice isn't always spoken. Sometimes it's a text. Sometimes it's a feeling. Sometimes it’s the whisper of doubt.

Ignore it. Delete the message. Block the number.

Leo stared at the screen. His thumb hovered. He deleted the message.

He heard his mom's voice again. From the kitchen.

[shout] Leo! Your pizza's getting cold, sweetie!

He went down. Ate dinner. His mom seemed normal. Happy.

He went to bed at 10:30 PM.

Rule two. Don't look out the window.

The thing outside wants you to look. It wants to be seen. It wants you to acknowledge it.

Don't give it that power. Keep the blinds shut. Keep the curtains drawn.

Leo’s blinds were already closed. He felt a weird chill. Like someone was watching.

He tried to sleep. At 11:40 PM, his phone buzzed again. Another text. From Liam.

"I can see you."

Leo gasped. He hadn't unblocked Liam. How?

He deleted the message again. This time, he turned off his phone. Completely.

Rule three. Don't acknowledge its existence.

It feeds on attention. On fear. On recognition.

Pretend it isn't there. Don't talk about it. Don't think about it.

It's just the wind. It's just an old house. It's just your imagination.

Leo pulled the covers tight. He squeezed his eyes shut. His heart pounded.

He heard a faint tapping. At his window. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Rule four. Don't open the door.

Or the window. Or the closet. If something wants in, it will try every opening.

It will try to trick you. To tempt you. To scare you.

The tapping grew louder. Then a scratch. Like fingernails on glass.

Leo buried his head in his pillow. He prayed for morning.

At 3:02 AM, he heard it. A soft, familiar voice. From just outside his door.

[whisper] Leo? Sweetie? Are you awake? It's Mom.

Rule five. Don't answer the call.

It can mimic voices. Perfectly. Your mom. Your dad. Your friend. Even your pet.

Don't respond. Don't make a sound. Don't move.

Leo froze. His mom always slept like a log. This was wrong.

[whisper] Leo, open the door. I made you hot chocolate. You had a bad dream.

His mom never made hot chocolate in the middle of the night.

[whisper] Come on, Leo. Don't be silly. It's just me.

He stayed perfectly still. Silent. Waiting. The voice persisted for minutes. Then it stopped.

He heard slow, heavy footsteps. Moving away from his door. Down the stairs.

Rule six. Don't let it know you know.

It wants to play a game. It wants you to be a participant.

Never let it know you're aware of its presence. Your silence is your shield.

He heard the front door creak open. Then shut.

Then… silence.

Leo lay awake. For hours. Waiting for dawn. The first hint of gray light.

He crept out of bed. Tiptoed to the window. Peeking through the blinds.

The sun was rising. Everything looked normal. The street. The neighbor's house.

Liam's bike was leaning against his front porch. Right there.

That's when he remembered. Liam's bike was stolen last week.

And Liam never called him "sweetie." His mom did.

Rule seven. The one nobody finds in time.

There was no "it." There was no "thing" outside.

There was only a test. A test of your loyalty.

The Rules weren't for *your* safety. They were for *its* protection.

You were supposed to break them. For your friend. For your family.

You were supposed to open the door. To look outside. To answer the call.

Because the real monster… was the rules themselves.

And by following them, you trapped yourself. You locked out the ones who truly needed help.

Leo heard a sound. From downstairs. A soft, whimpering cry.

His mom.

[face_mom_cry]

His phone buzzed. One last text. Not from Liam. From his mom.

"He's inside, Leo. He's been inside all night."

Don't look out the window. Don't open the door. Don't answer the call.

You just sealed their fate. Not yours.

Sleep tight.
